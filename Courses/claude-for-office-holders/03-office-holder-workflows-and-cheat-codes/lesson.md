Welcome back to Runtime Republic. In Episode 2 we set the ground rules. Today we put AI to work on the real tasks of public office: five workflows you can use this week, and six cheat codes, short phrases that make every answer more useful.

All of them follow one pattern: read, question, verify, decide. The assistant helps you read faster and question better. You verify against the official documents. You decide.

Workflow one: the agenda packet.

The packet arrives Thursday for a Tuesday meeting, and it is ninety pages. Give the public document to Claude and ask: "List every action item. For each, tell me what we are being asked to approve, how much it costs, which fund pays for it, and whether it is a one-time or recurring cost." Then: "Which items have the least supporting information?" Those are the items where your questions will matter most. You still read the packet. Now you read it with a map.

Workflow two: the budget.

Budgets are where priorities become real, and they are hard to read. Ask Claude to help you compare this year's proposed budget to last year's: "Which lines grew the most, in dollars and in percentage? Which are new? Where did a fund balance drop?" Then turn the answers into questions for staff, not conclusions. A good budget question is specific and fair: "This line grew eighteen percent. What is driving that, and what happens if we hold it flat?" Staff will appreciate a precise question more than a vague complaint, and so will the public.

Workflow three: bills and ordinances.

For a state bill, give Claude the bill text and its fiscal note and ask: "Explain what this would change in plain English. Which current statutes does it amend? Who is affected? What does the fiscal note say it will cost, and what assumptions does it rely on?" For a local ordinance, ask how similar cities have handled the same issue, and then check those examples yourself. Ask, too, about unintended consequences. A provision written for one situation often reaches others.

Workflow four: constituent mail.

Many offices receive more messages than a small staff can answer well. Claude can help you sort messages by topic and draft a clear, accurate reply on each issue, which you then edit and personalize. Remember the rules from Episode 2: keep personal details out of any tool your body has not approved, and read every word before it goes out. A reply that shows you actually read the letter is worth more than a fast one.

Workflow five: explaining your decisions.

After a hard vote, constituents deserve a clear explanation, especially the ones who disagree. Ask Claude: "Help me explain this vote in under two hundred words: what the decision was, the main reasons, the strongest objection, and how I weighed it." Then make it yours. An honest explanation that acknowledges the other side earns respect even from people who would have voted differently.

Now for the cheat codes. Add these to almost any question.

Cheat code one: "What does it cost, and who pays?" Every proposal has a price and someone who bears it. This keeps both in view.

Cheat code two: "What questions should I ask staff?" Turns a long document into a short list of the questions that matter.

Cheat code three: "Make the strongest case against this." Ask for the best argument from the other side, not a straw man. If you cannot answer it, you are not ready to vote.

Cheat code four: "What evidence is missing?" Shows you what a proposal does not tell you, which is often the most important part.

Cheat code five: "Explain this to a constituent in one hundred fifty words." Forces clarity. If it cannot be explained simply, ask why.

Cheat code six: "What could go wrong?" Ask about unintended consequences, edge cases and who might fall through the cracks. A minute of this before a vote can save a year of fixes after.

A word about staff. These tools do not replace clerks, analysts, attorneys and department heads. They make your time with them better. Arrive at a briefing having already read the document and with sharper questions, and you will get better answers.

Let's recap. Read, question, verify, decide. Five workflows: agenda packets, budgets, bills and ordinances, constituent mail, and explaining your decisions. And six cheat codes to keep cost, evidence and consequences in view.

So far, everything in this series is something you do in your own office. In Episode 4, we look outward: how citizens are starting to use these same tools to organize, research and propose, and how office holders can meet them where that work is happening.

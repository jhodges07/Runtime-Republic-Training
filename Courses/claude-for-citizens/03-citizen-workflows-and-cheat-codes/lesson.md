Welcome back to Runtime Republic. In Episode 2 we covered the habits. Today we put them to work. Five citizen workflows you can use this week, and six cheat codes, short phrases that make every answer better.

First, a simple pattern that runs through all of them. Find, understand, check, act.

Find the official document. Understand it with Claude's help. Check what Claude told you against the original. Then act: share it, ask a question, or speak up.

Let's go.

Workflow one: read a bill in ten minutes.

Your state legislature posts every bill online, usually with a bill number like House Bill 2100. Find the bill on the official legislature website. Copy the text, or the link, into Claude and ask: "Summarize this bill in plain English. What would it change? Who would it affect? What does it cost, if the fiscal note says?" Then ask the follow-up that matters most: "Which sections should I read myself?" Open those sections and read them. Ten minutes, and you understand a bill better than most people commenting on it.

Workflow two: follow a tax dollar.

Pull out your property tax statement and black out your name, address and account numbers. Look at the list of taxing bodies: the county, the city, the school district, and others, each with its own rate. Ask Claude: "Explain each line on a Kansas property tax statement, and what a mill levy is." Then pick one line, say your school district, and ask Claude to help you find that district's published budget. Now you can see where your dollar actually goes. Do this once a month, five minutes at a time, and in a year you will know your local government better than almost anyone.

Workflow three: check a viral post.

Someone shares a post on Facebook or X with a shocking claim about a law or a budget. Before you share it, copy the claim into Claude and ask: "Is this accurate? Find the original source, with a link and a date. What context is missing?" Then open the source. Sometimes the post is right. Often it is half right, out of date, or missing the part that changes the story. This works no matter which side the post comes from, and it is the single best thing you can do to keep your own feed honest.

Workflow four: get ready to contact your representative.

Ask Claude to help you find out who represents you. Your state house member, your state senator, and your members of Congress. Then ask it to help you organize your thoughts: "I'm concerned about this bill for these reasons. Help me outline a short, respectful message with one clear request." Then write the message yourself, in your own words, and keep it short. A real message from a real constituent in their district carries weight. A copied paragraph does not.

Workflow five: understand a public meeting.

City councils, county commissions and school boards post agendas before their meetings and minutes afterward. Paste the agenda into Claude and ask: "What decisions are on this agenda? Which items involve spending money? What questions would a careful citizen ask?" Now you know which meeting to attend, which item to watch, and what to ask if you speak during public comment.

Now for the cheat codes. These are short phrases you can add to almost any question.

Cheat code one: "Explain it like I'm new." This gets rid of jargon and gives you the basics first.

Cheat code two: "What's the source, and what's the date?" Every fact you plan to repeat should have both.

Cheat code three: "What would the other side say?" This gives you the strongest argument against your view, so you are ready for it.

Cheat code four: "What's missing?" Claude will point out what a document, post or argument leaves out. Often that is the most important part.

Cheat code five: "Give me three questions to ask." Use it before a meeting, a phone call, or a vote. Good questions move officials more than speeches.

Cheat code six: "Check my reasoning." Paste in what you plan to say and ask Claude to find weak spots, wrong facts, or anything unfair. It is much easier to hear that from a machine at midnight than from a commissioner at a public meeting.

A word about tone. Claude can help you be clear, but you set the spirit. Be firm about the facts and respectful to the people. Officials are neighbors too, and the citizens who get listened to are the ones who show up prepared and stay civil.

Let's recap. Find, understand, check, act. Five workflows: read a bill, follow a tax dollar, check a viral post, contact your representative, and understand a public meeting. And six cheat codes to make every answer better.

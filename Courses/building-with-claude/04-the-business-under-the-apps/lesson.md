Welcome back to Runtime Republic. We have covered history, habits and workflows. Today is about the foundation underneath all of it: the business itself. First, getting your LLC current and complete. Then, a business you can start this month: teaching small business owners how to get the most out of Claude.

A quick note before we start. I am not a lawyer or an accountant. Treat this as a checklist to bring to the official sources and, where needed, to a professional.

Let's start with why this matters. Your phone app depends on it. Every release, starting with the very first alpha, will ship from the LLC's own Apple developer account, so testers never have to switch over later. To enroll an organization in the Apple Developer Program, Apple checks that the company legally exists and that its name matches its records. That usually means a D-U-N-S number, a free business identifier from Dun and Bradstreet, under the exact legal name of your LLC. If the LLC is not in good standing, or the name does not match, your first alpha waits. So the paperwork is not a side chore. It is on the critical path.

Here is the checklist, in order. Think of it as CONTROL zero for the business.

Step one: check your standing. If your LLC is registered in Kansas, search for it on the Kansas Secretary of State's business entity search. Look at the status. Active and in good standing is what you want. If it says delinquent or forfeited, you have work to do, and that is fine. Kansas has a reinstatement process.

Step two: catch up on annual reports. Kansas LLCs file a report every year, due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month after your fiscal year ends. For most people, that means April fifteenth. File any you have missed, pay the fees, and reinstate if needed. Then put next year's deadline on your calendar, with a reminder a month ahead.

Step three: confirm your registered agent and addresses. The state needs a reliable address where legal notices reach you. If you have moved, update it.

Step four: get your operating agreement in writing. Even a single-member LLC should have one. It says who owns the company, how decisions are made, and what happens if something changes. Claude can help you draft one from a template, and a lawyer can review it.

Step five: separate the money. Get an Employer Identification Number from the IRS. It is free, and it takes minutes online. Open a business bank account under the LLC's name. Pay for your Apple developer fee, your domains, and your Claude subscription from that account. Keeping business and personal money apart protects you, and makes tax time far easier.

Step six: tie your brands to the company. RuntimeRepublic.com and BlueprintLiberty.com should be registered to the LLC, or at least paid by it. If you sell under those names rather than the LLC's legal name, look into registering a trade name.

Step seven: set up simple books. A spreadsheet is enough to start. Income, expenses, receipts, every month. Claude can build you the sheet and categorize your transactions.

One more item. There was a federal rule requiring small companies to report their beneficial owners. In 2025, the Treasury Department exempted companies formed in the United States. Check the current rules when you file, because this one has changed more than once.

Now, here is the cheat code for all seven steps. Put them in a GitHub repository. A control document for the business. Each step is an issue. Close each issue when it is done, with a note on what you filed and when. Next year, you are not starting over. You have a record.

Alright. Now the fun part. Consulting.

Here is the truth about small businesses and AI right now. Almost every owner has heard of it. Very few use it well. They try it once, get a generic answer, and decide it is a toy. What they need is not a lecture on AI. They need someone to sit beside them and set it up for their actual business.

That someone can be you. You bring thirty-nine years of aerospace discipline. You know how to write a procedure that works the same way every time. That is exactly what small businesses lack.

Here is a simple offer ladder.

Rung one: the Claude Kickstart. A two-hour session, in person or on a video call. You learn the owner's business, set up a Claude project with their instructions and files, and build three workflows they will use tomorrow. Answering customer emails. Writing their weekly social posts. Turning invoices into a monthly report.

Rung two: the thirty-day build. You document their three most painful processes as plain-language control documents, turn each into a saved skill, and connect Claude to the tools they already use, like their email, calendar or shared drive. At the end, they own a playbook.

Rung three: monthly office hours. A standing call where you review what is working, fix what is not, and add one new workflow each month.

A few rules to keep it honest. Work in the client's own accounts, so they own everything you build. Never promise specific savings you have not measured. Teach them to verify. And protect their data like it is your own.

Your marketing writes itself. Publish what you build in public: this audio series, your essays, your foreclosure dashboard. Each one proves you know how to make Claude do real work. And run all of this through the LLC, so the consulting income helps fund the apps.

Let's recap. Get the LLC in good standing, because your app launch depends on it. Track the business paperwork like a project. And start consulting with a simple three-rung offer.
